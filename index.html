Career Aspirations

My career goal is to become a Quality Assurance (QA) Engineer, specialising in software testing and quality control. I was inspired by a workshop delivered as part of the Professional Development (ISYS10401) module at my university, which explored software testing careers and the role of QA in the software development lifecycle. This introduced me to the importance of verification, validation, and the prevention of software faults in real-world systems (ISTQB, 2018).

I am currently studying for a BSc in Computer Science at Nottingham Trent University as a first-year undergraduate. I hold A-levels in Maths, Business, and PE (grades A*AA), which have strengthened my logical reasoning, attention to detail, and ability to perform under pressure. I also serve as a TAP Ambassador, which has developed my communication skills and professionalism in representing the university.

I am actively building skills in manual testing, agile practices, and test automation, with a long-term goal to specialise in test automation using tools like Selenium and Cypress. I have completed over 30 hours of Continuing Professional Development (CPD), including ISTQB Foundation Level exam preparation and skill-building via LinkedIn Learning, BCS.org, and Udemy.

Over the next 2–5 years, I aim to complete my degree, gain QA-focused internship experience, and pursue further specialisation. I have also explored roles such as Software Test Analyst and Test Automation Engineer (Prospects.ac.uk, 2024), understanding the technical, analytical, and soft skills required for each. These include proficiency in bug-tracking tools (e.g., JIRA), scripting and test frameworks, and collaborative communication with developers and stakeholders (Graham, 2008).

This career path aligns with my natural strengths in detail orientation, logical reasoning, and ensuring software reliability, as well as my interest in protecting users through robust testing processes.

Reflection on Professional Conduct

Professional conduct is an essential aspect of the IT profession and has been central to my development during the ISYS10401 module. According to the British Computer Society (BCS, 2023), professionalism in computing includes ethical behaviour, accountability, integrity, and contributing to public good. I have reflected on these principles and actively applied them in my academic and personal conduct.

I consistently attended all sessions punctually and prepared, which reflects accountability and respect for peers and educators. In group work, I engaged respectfully, welcomed feedback, and contributed meaningfully — reflecting the BCS principle of working with honesty and fairness.

I adhered to academic integrity policies by referencing sources, avoiding plagiarism, and submitting work on time. This links to professional competence and public interest, both outlined in the BCS Code of Conduct (BCS, 2023).

My role as a TAP Ambassador has further developed my communication, leadership, and ethical judgement, particularly when mentoring prospective students. Additionally, my upcoming summer internship teaching computing will challenge me to apply professional values in a new environment, developing my ability to adapt to different teams, communicate technical ideas clearly, and maintain high personal standards.

Going forward, I intend to continue building professionalism by maintaining ethical decision-making, upholding industry standards, and seeking feedback for continuous improvement — all essential for a future QA Engineer (Martin, 2002).
